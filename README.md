# MariaDB Foundation hackathons

The MariaDB Foundation arranges hackathons, and hosts challenges at other organisers' hackathons, to help people
adopt MariaDB and to encourage contributions to it. Participants build with MariaDB, learn the newest features, and
get their work seen. We get reference implementations, integrations and feedback that make MariaDB better for
everyone.

Projects usually fall into two tracks:

- **Integration:** make MariaDB easy to access and use in every tool, language and environment.
- **Innovation:** build feature demos and use cases that others can find, learn from and adapt.

Everything about our hackathons, past and future, is on **[mariadb.org/hackathon](https://mariadb.org/hackathon)**.
To organise a hackathon with us, write to [hackathon@mariadb.org](mailto:hackathon@mariadb.org).

This repository keeps the results: one folder per hackathon, with a list of the submissions and links to the teams'
own repositories.

## Hackathons

A full list of the hackathons the MariaDB Foundation has taken part in, with their blogs and winners, is at
[mariadb.org/hackathons](https://mariadb.org/hackathons).

## Junction 2026

[Junction](https://2026.hackjunction.com/) is Europe's biggest hackathon, held every November in Espoo, Finland. Over
2,000 people build for 48 hours on challenges from companies and organisations. At Junction 2026, on 13–15
November, the MariaDB Foundation hosted **MariaDB's Wikipedia challenge**: build the tools Wikipedia is missing, using
MariaDB Vector, system-versioned tables, or both. Wikipedia itself runs on MariaDB.

36 teams submitted a project. The winners were:

1. **Janus**, by Saunan Pojat: marks where Wikipedia's language editions disagree, right in the article
   ([code](https://github.com/ayaanjamil/janus))
2. **Language Mirror**, by Bob the Builders: compares how every language edition tells the same story over time
   ([code](https://github.com/silvekoski/languagemirror))
3. **Ghosts of Wikipedia**, by Chrysalis: brings deleted sentences back to life, searchable by meaning
   ([code](https://github.com/salomeshioshvili/ghosts_of_wikipedia))

- **All submissions:** [junction-2026/submissions.csv](junction-2026/submissions.csv) lists every project, with links
  to its code, public video and demo, and what we found in its repository: how it uses MariaDB, the stack and the
  licence.
- **What the teams started from:** the
  [mariadb-wikipedia-datasets](https://github.com/MariaDB/mariadb-wikipedia-datasets) (Finnish, Swedish and English
  Wikipedia, with the full edit history and ready-made vectors), and two starter repositories,
  [mariadb-vector-wikipedia-rag](https://github.com/MariaDB/mariadb-vector-wikipedia-rag) and
  [mariadb-system-versioning-wikipedia](https://github.com/MariaDB/mariadb-system-versioning-wikipedia).
